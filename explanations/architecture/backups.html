Backups and disaster recovery#

Git is the backup for configuration. Every manifest, chart version, role and sealed credential is in this repository, and ArgoCD rebuilds the cluster’s contents from it. So the backup system only has to cover what Git cannot hold: application data, the cluster’s own state, and a handful of keys.

This page is the overview: what is backed up, when, to where, and which runbook brings it back. The procedures themselves are in Backup and restore and Recover the control plane.

The shape of it#

Backups move outwards in three steps, and every step that leaves the house is encrypted to a key the cluster does not have. The NAS backups leave it twice: to Google Drive, and to a Backblaze B2 bucket where nothing, not even someone who has taken over the cluster, can delete them for 30 days.

  1. To the NAS. Each stateful workload has a job that writes a consistent copy to /bigdisk/k8s-cluster/backups/ on the NAS. The NAS is a different machine from every node, so this covers a dead disk or a dead node.

  2. Off the NAS. scrap’s newest backup is also copied to nuc2’s local disk, and everything under backups/ is uploaded to Google Drive and to Backblaze B2. That covers a dead NAS, and B2’s lock covers an attacker who deletes Drive and the NAS together.

  3. Encrypted to an offline key. The etcd bundles are encrypted before they reach the NAS, and everything is encrypted before it reaches Drive or B2, with age to one public key. The private key is not on the cluster, the NAS, Drive or B2.

Three things in that table deserve a reason:

  • Prometheus is not backed up. Metrics refill by scraping, a consistent copy needs the snapshot API, and the history is not worth that machinery. Its volume still survives a cluster rebuild.

  • scrap has three layers because it is the one application whose data cannot be recreated. The Litestream replica makes a node loss cost about a second. The nightly runs are point-in-time copies of the database and the files together. The copy on nuc2 exists because the live data, the replica and the backups are all on the same NAS.

  • etcd is backed up outside Kubernetes. A systemd timer on the control plane keeps working when the API server does not, which is exactly when the backup matters. K3s also takes its own snapshots twice a day, but they stay on node02 and are lost with it.

The off-site copies#

The offsite-backup CronJob (kubernetes-services/additions/backups/, settings in offsite_backup in values.yaml) uploads to the folder home-cluster-offsite in a personal Google Drive with rclone. The offsite-backup-b2 CronJob runs the same scripts against a Backblaze B2 bucket, for the NAS backups only (offsite_backup.b2.include); each target’s include list says what it gets. Their rules are what make them safe to leave running:

  • Encrypted first. Each file is streamed through age straight into the upload, so nothing readable is staged anywhere. Files that are already .age (the etcd bundles) go up as they are.

  • Add only. It never mirrors a deletion. A backup that the NAS’s own retention removes, or that a wiped or encrypted NAS loses, stays on Drive.

  • Cautious pruning. A file on Drive is deleted only when its source has been gone from the NAS for more than prune_days (180) and it is not among the keep_min (4) newest of its series. An empty backups/ (a missing mount, a wiped NAS) stops the job before it uploads or prunes.

  • A size check on Minecraft. A nightly tarball smaller than min_size_percent (50) of the largest one on Drive fails the run and stops pruning, so a wiped source cannot push the good copies out.

  • Least privilege. rclone’s Drive token has the drive.file scope: it sees only files it created. It can delete those, and someone with root on the cluster can read it and delete the NAS backups too. That used to be an accepted risk; the B2 copy now covers it. The B2 bucket was made with Object Lock in compliance mode and a 30-day default retention, so no key, not even the account owner’s, can delete or overwrite a version, or shorten its lock, within 30 days of its upload. The job’s own key is also narrow: restricted to the bucket, with no capability that touches retention, legal holds, buckets or keys. Its pruning only hides files, and the bucket’s lifecycle rule deletes a hidden version once its lock has expired (14. A Second, Immutable Off-site Copy on Backblaze B2, Security model).

  • Three identities. The NAS squashes root and no single user can read everything, so three containers run side by side: backups as nobody for the etcd bundles and tarballs, scrap as uid 10001 for scrap’s runs, and minecraft as uid 1000 for the worlds.

What does not go off site: scrap’s live data volume (only its backup runs do), the local-nvme volumes themselves (only the backups made from them), other nfs-stable claims, the OpenTofu state, and the media library.

Every job is timed around the nodes’ own maintenance: the etcd backup before anything changes, the backups to the NAS in the gap before the windows that drain nodes, and the off-site copy after the last of them.

The order is the design. The etcd backup runs before anything changes the nodes, so there is always a snapshot from before the night’s patching. The off-site job runs last so that it carries that night’s backups, not the previous night’s. The scrap and Grafana jobs, and scrap’s copy to nuc2, run between 00:30 and 01:00, after unattended-upgrades (which never drains or reboots) and before the two windows that do. jellyfin-daily stays at 01:45, after Jellyfin’s own 01:00 tasks: a K3s patch round that drains nuc2 then delays it, and a job interrupted that way is retried.

Knowing when a backup failed#

  • etcd: the backup script writes k3s_etcd_backup_* metrics through node-exporter’s textfile collector. Four rules in additions/grafana/etcd-backup-rules.yaml fire on a failed run, a bundle older than 36 hours, no success ever, or missing metrics. Alertmanager pushes these to the phone through ntfy.

  • Off-site: OffsiteBackupFailed, OffsiteBackupStale and OffsiteBackupNeverSucceeded (offsite-backup-rules.yaml) fire from kube-state-metrics, per CronJob, so a failure on Drive or on B2 alerts by its own name. They are warnings on the push list, so like the etcd alerts they reach the phone through ntfy.

  • The CronJobs to the NAS (Grafana, Jellyfin, scrap): BackupJobFailed, BackupJobStale and BackupJobNeverSucceeded (backup-job-rules.yaml) fire on a failed run, a last success older than the schedule allows, or no success ever, and are pushed to the phone too.

  • scrap has a cross-check: scrap-backup-copy fails if the newest complete run is more than 26 hours old, so a backup that silently stopped shows up as a failed Job the next morning.

Which alerts are pushed, and how to add one, is in Monitoring and alerts.

Two cases need no restore at all:

  • A planned rebuild (pb_decommission.yml, then pb_all.yml) keeps the local volumes and re-binds scrap’s NFS volumes and the worlds’ nfs-stable folders. The sealing key does not survive it: the new cluster makes its own, and the rebuild seals every secret again under that key (Re-flash and rebuild). The reasoning is on the storage page.

  • Losing Drive loses only the third copy: the NAS backups are on B2 too.

One loss needs a different start: an attacker who deleted Drive and the NAS backups (anyone with root on the cluster can). B2 still holds every version uploaded in the last 30 days: take the cluster’s keys away, read the bucket as it was before the attack, and rebuild from it (After an attacker wiped Drive and the NAS).

The one loss with no recovery is the age private key together with the cluster: the encrypted backups then cannot be read. That is why the key is kept offline in two places, and why proving a bundle decrypts is part of the setup in Nightly encrypted off-node backup.
