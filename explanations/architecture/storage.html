Storage#

The cluster has no replicated block storage. A few workloads that need a fast local disk (SQLite databases and Prometheus’s time series) each get a plain directory on one named node, and everything else lives on the NAS over NFS. A workload on a local disk is tied to that node and is down when the node is; in exchange there is no storage system to run, no replica traffic on 8 GB nodes, and all data survives a full rebuild of the cluster. The decision and what it replaced (Longhorn) are recorded in ADR 0012.

This page describes where every byte is. Procedures are in the how-tos it links to.

Local disks: local-nvme#

A local-nvme volume is a directory, and four separate things have to agree about it.

        flowchart TB
    gv["group_vars/all.yml<br/>k8s_data_dirs: node, path, owner, mode"]
    role["k8s_data_dirs role<br/>(servers play)"]
    dir["Directory on the node<br/>/var/lib/k8s-data/grafana on node04"]

    val["values.yaml<br/>local_storage: node, path, claim"]
    app["local-storage app<br/>sync wave -5"]
    pv["PersistentVolume grafana-local<br/>nodeAffinity: node04<br/>claimRef: storage-grafana-prometheus-0"]

    pvc["PersistentVolumeClaim<br/>made by the workload's chart"]
    pod["Pod, scheduled on node04"]

    gv --> role --> dir
    val --> app --> pv
    pvc -- "binds, by claimRef" --> pv
    pv -- "local path" --> dir
    pod --> pvc

  • The directory is created by the k8s_data_dirs role from the k8s_data_dirs list in group_vars/all.yml, on the entry’s node only, with the owner and mode the pod’s securityContext expects. It is the only thing in the repository that creates these directories.

  • The volume comes from local_storage in kubernetes-services/values.yaml: the local-storage app renders one PersistentVolume per entry, with nodeAffinity for the node and a claimRef naming the exact claim that will use it.

  • The claim is created later by the workload’s own chart (Grafana, Prometheus) or by a small template beside it (Jellyfin, scrap). Because the volume already reserves that claim’s name, the claim binds to it and to nothing else. Deleting an Application leaves its claim: the templates carry Prune=false,Delete=false, and ArgoCD does not track the claims the StatefulSets make. Deleting a claim by hand leaves its volume Released; see A local-nvme PV stuck Released.

  • The pod follows the volume: the class binds WaitForFirstConsumer, so the scheduler places the pod on the node the volume is pinned to.

The local-storage app syncs in wave -5, ahead of everything else. If a chart created its claim first, the claim would have no volume waiting and would fall through to the default class, nfs-stable, and Grafana would start on an empty NFS folder.

The SYNC contract. node and path appear twice, once for Ansible and once for Helm, and must match. If they drift the volume points at a directory that does not exist and the pod never starts. Both files carry a SYNC: comment saying so.

One volume, one workload, one chosen host. There is no shared pool. A new workload that needs a local disk gets its own entry in both lists and an explicit node. Current placement:

These paths are sacred. /var/lib/k8s-data/* on the RK1s and /home/k8s-data/* on nuc2 sit outside /var/lib/rancher deliberately. pb_decommission.yml removes K3s and everything under /var/lib/rancher, and leaves these directories alone, so a rebuilt cluster finds Grafana’s dashboards and Jellyfin’s database where they were. Only -e wipe_local_data=true deletes them. On an RK1 the directory is on the NVMe that also holds the root filesystem, so a forced re-flash (flash_force) of node04 wipes it: that is a deliberate, announced act, not part of a rebuild. Moving a volume to another node is a runbook: Move a local-nvme PV to another node.

The NAS#

The NAS is a QNAP at 192.168.1.3. Ansible never touches it: it also holds personal data, so its directories are created once by hand (Set up the cluster’s directories on the NAS) and the cluster is only ever an NFS client.

/bigdisk/
  k8s-cluster/              the cluster's own tree (nfs.cluster_share_path)
    scrap-data/             scrap: files, preview cache, database replica
    minecraft/              dashboard store: backups, snapshots, registry
    backups/
      grafana/  jellyfin/   daily tarballs, and weekly/ below each
      scrap/                scrap's backup runs, and monthly/
      scrap-curated/        scrap runs kept by hand
      etcd/                 encrypted etcd bundles and sealing keys
  media/                    Jellyfin's library, mounted read-only
  minecraft-archive/        precious world archive, read-only
/k8s-dynamic/               NFS CSI share (nfs_csi.share): nfs-rwx volumes
  stable/                   nfs-stable volumes (nfs_csi.stable_share)
    minecraft/<world>-data  one folder per claim
    archived-minecraft/     folders of deleted claims

Two of those are protected by where they are:

  • /bigdisk/media is mounted read-only three times over (the volume, its access mode and the pod’s mount), so Jellyfin cannot alter the library.

  • /bigdisk/minecraft-archive is outside k8s-cluster/ on purpose. No tool that works on “the cluster’s share” can reach it, it is read-only on the NAS and in the mount, and the dashboard only ever reads it.

nfs-stable: a folder that depends only on the claim’s name#

The NFS CSI driver normally names a volume’s folder after the volume, which is a random ID. nfs-stable names it <namespace>/<claim> instead, and on delete it archives the folder and does not remove it (onDelete: archive).

        flowchart TB
    create["Claim minecraft/science-lab-data created"]
    live["Folder stable/minecraft/science-lab-data"]
    rebuild["Cluster rebuilt:<br/>every PV and PVC object gone,<br/>folder untouched"]
    again["Claim re-created with the same name"]
    deleted["Claim deleted on purpose"]
    archived["Folder moved to<br/>stable/archived-minecraft/science-lab-data"]
    fresh["A new claim of that name<br/>starts empty"]

    create --> live
    live --> rebuild --> again
    again -- "binds to the same folder" --> live
    live --> deleted --> archived --> fresh

That one rule is what carries dynamic volumes across a rebuild. A rebuild wipes etcd, so the cluster forgets every volume, but the folders are still on the NAS under names that can be worked out again. The Minecraft dashboard keeps a registry of its worlds on its store; after a rebuild it re-creates each world, the claims come back under the same names, and they bind to the old data.

The other side of the rule: deleting a claim never deletes data, and the driver keeps only one archive per name. An old archive has to be cleared on the NAS before a claim of the same name is deleted again. The details are in Stable NFS volumes.

scrap: a database that is not on NFS#

SQLite on NFS is unsafe, and a local volume would pin scrap to one node. So scrap does neither (ADR 0013):

  • the database is on the pod’s emptyDir, on whichever node the pod is on;

  • Litestream replicates it, about a second behind, to /data/litestream on scrap-data-nfs;

  • uploaded files and the preview cache are ordinary files on the same volume;

  • every new pod’s init container restores the database from the replica before scrap starts, and refuses to start empty if the volume looks wrong.

scrap therefore has no node selector. It restarts on any node after a drain, and after a node is fenced. Both of its volumes are static NFS volumes bound to fixed NAS directories, with Prune=false,Delete=false, so neither a rebuild nor an ArgoCD mistake can detach them from their data.

Note

scrap-local (/home/k8s-data/scrap on nuc2) is scrap’s volume from before that change, kept as a frozen copy. Nothing mounts it. Its entries stay in local_storage and k8s_data_dirs because removing them would prune a volume that still has a bound claim.

Ephemeral on purpose#

Some state is deliberately not kept:

  • ntfy keeps its user database and message cache on an emptyDir. The users are provisioned from configuration at every start, and the cache only serves phones that were offline. Alerts are often about a node going down, so the pod must be free to move.

  • Jellyfin’s cache and transcodes are a 20Gi emptyDir (jellyfin.cache_size).

  • Alertmanager has no volume: silences and notification state do not survive a pod restart.

What this design does not give#

  • No failover for pinned workloads. If node04 is down there is no Grafana or Prometheus; if nuc2 is down there is no Jellyfin and no Minecraft. Moving one is manual.

  • No replication of live local data. Between two nightly backups, a dead disk loses up to a day of Grafana or Jellyfin changes.

  • The NAS is a single point for scrap, the Minecraft worlds and every first-level backup. It is one box, not a cluster.

  • No snapshots, no volume UI, no automatic rebalancing when nodes come and go.

  • Capacity is not enforced. A volume can fill the disk under it.

  • A volume cannot be resized or moved by Kubernetes. Moving one means copying a directory and editing two lists.

For a homelab with one administrator these are the right things to give up: what remains is directories and NFS folders that can be read with ls, and a cluster that can be torn down and rebuilt without losing anything.
