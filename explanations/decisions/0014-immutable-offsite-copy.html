14. A Second, Immutable Off-site Copy on Backblaze B2#

Status: Accepted

Context#

Every backup lands on the NAS, and a nightly job copies it, age-encrypted, to Google Drive. The Drive token has the drive.file scope, so it sees only the files it made, but it can delete them. So can anything that holds it. The token is in a Secret in the backups namespace, and the NAS backups are on a share the cluster mounts. Someone with root on the cluster, or a compromised agent with cluster access, could read the token and delete the Drive copy and the NAS copies together. The age encryption keeps them from reading the backups, not from destroying them. Until now that was an accepted risk.

Making the Drive copy undeletable is not possible with a consumer Google account: the job’s own pruning needs delete rights, and Drive has no retention lock outside Google Workspace.

Decision#

Add a second off-site target: a Backblaze B2 bucket with Object Lock in compliance mode and a default retention of 30 days. Keep Drive as it is, as the first target.

  • The same job. A second CronJob, offsite-backup-b2, runs the same scripts with the same age key, the same names and layout, and the same pruning. Each target is its own CronJob with its own Secret, so a failure on one alerts by its name and never stops the other’s uploads.

  • Only the NAS backups. A per-target include list picks what goes. B2 gets backups/; the Minecraft tarball and archive stay on Drive (the archive has other copies). That keeps B2 at tens of GB and a few dollars a year.

  • Compliance mode, 30 days. No key, not even the account owner’s master key, can delete or overwrite a locked version or shorten its retention. 30 days is far longer than tampering would go unnoticed: the OffsiteBackupFailed and OffsiteBackupStale alerts fire within 36 hours of a stopped or failing job. Longer would cost more for every upload and cannot be undone.

  • Pruning hides. rclone’s deletions on B2 hide files (hard_delete stays false), and a lifecycle rule deletes a hidden version a day after hiding, once its lock has expired. So the job’s pruning frees space as on Drive, while any version uploaded in the last 30 days stays recoverable, whenever it was hidden or overwritten.

  • A narrow key. The job’s application key is restricted to the bucket, with listBuckets, listFiles, readFiles, writeFiles and deleteFiles only. scripts/seal-b2-offsite checks that with B2 before sealing it.

  • Off by default. offsite_backup.b2.enabled is false until the owner has made the account, the bucket and the key.

Consequences#

  • Someone who takes over the cluster can delete Drive and the NAS, but not the last 30 days of uploads on B2. Recovery reads the bucket as it was before the attack (--b2-version-at).

  • What is immutable is what was uploaded in the last 30 days. Older files are past their lock and can be deleted with the job’s key. An attacker who silences the job and the alerts and waits over 30 days could still destroy everything; there is no external dead-man switch.

  • The uploads skip the temporary .partial- name on B2 (a rename is a copy there, and the copy would be locked too), so the script holds the stream open until the pipeline’s status is known.

  • One more account, key and SealedSecret to keep; the rebuild exports and reseals the key with the other external credentials.

  • Restores, and the restore test, can read either target (just restore-test --target b2).
